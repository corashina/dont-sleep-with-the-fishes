# Visual Style Guide

## Identity
Create an illustrated, weathered sea world. Combine dark comedy with lonely maritime life.
Show loneliness through weather, distance, empty space, and worn human objects.
Show comedy through odd forms, contrast, timing, and small stage details. Avoid bright or elastic comedy.
Use the original game only as a character reference. Do not copy its art, icons, fonts, textures, or layouts.

## Forms and materials
Build clear stylized forms with useful detail, asymmetry, and local wear. Low-poly work must look complete.
Use seams, joints, handles, bevels, and layers to explain form and use. Keep believable weight and attachment.
Make each material distinct at play distance. Place wear where contact, water, salt, damage, and drainage cause it.
Avoid smooth primitive groups, perfect symmetry, random damage, uniform grime, and needless photo detail.

## UI
Keep the world dominant and the screen center open. Use sparse illustrated UI.
Place item details and actions near the item. Reserve full panels for journals, major choices, pauses, and endings.
Use clear type, stable numerals, and illustrated icons. Keep one material and stroke style across each component set.
Never use color alone for meaning. Keep all states, labels, keyboard focus, and unavailable reasons clear.
Do not add reduced-motion variants unless the owner requests them.

## Light, color, and composition
Light form and hierarchy first. Use ambient occlusion only for contacts, overlaps, seams, interiors, and crevices.
Favor cool sea and sky tones. Use warm brown, rust, and parchment tones for human objects.
Reserve small safety colors for interaction and comedy. Keep key subjects clear through value and material contrast.
Build clear foreground, midground, and horizon layers. Group props by story and silhouette.
Use empty space for distance and loneliness. Do not fill each gap with props, motion, or effects.
